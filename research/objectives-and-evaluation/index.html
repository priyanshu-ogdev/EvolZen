    Research Objectives & Evaluation Standard

    Status: Phase 0 scaffolding is complete and code baselines exist for Phase 1.1 (evolzen.environment, schemas/world.py, TransitionLogStore), Phase 1.2-numerical (agents/development/), and Phase 1.5 (evolzen.library); no empirical result is claimed yet.
    Scope: defines what EvolZen is trying to learn, what counts as evidence, and which comparisons must be completed before a paper claim is written.

    1. Target definition

    EvolZen does not target AGI, emotional intelligence, real-human psychology, or universal laws of civilization. Its target is a bounded, testable system with two separable capabilities:

    1. Persistent world growth: a seeded social sandbox changes durably through declared world processes and agent actions, producing attributable artifacts, capabilities, institutions, norms, markets, roles, and lineages without evaluator-injected milestones.
    2. Efficient adaptation: a predictor/recognition system adapts to held-out world seeds and mechanic combinations with fewer costly exploratory actions than declared baselines.

    World growth is not evidence of learning generality, and fresh-world adaptation is not evidence that the world itself is socially realistic. Both must be measured separately.

    2. Research questions

    The original developmental-agent question is specified in agent evaluation: do birth-time eight-drive equations, age/context modulation, personal learning and living-parent teaching produce useful adaptation or cultural accumulation beyond matched simpler controls? The new local code is a numerical baseline, not evidence of neural self-training. The detailed page supplies a proposed EAE formula, pilot budget, split rules, uncertainty unit and implementation gates.

    RQ1 — Can the world grow independently?

    Under a fixed transition contract, can the population produce durable world changes beyond a fixed scripted baseline, while preserving replayability, resource/institution invariants, and recovery after declared shocks?

    RQ2 — Does behavioral diversity matter?

    Does a typed heterogeneous population with QD selection produce more persistent specialization, lineage diversity, and world-growth coverage than a matched homogeneous or fitness-only population?

    RQ3 — Does verified exploration improve discovery?

    Does actor/synthesizer/verifier exploration improve held-out transition and intervention performance relative to passive exploration or a no-synthesis baseline, without increasing false promotions?

    RQ4 — Does the learning system improve adaptation?

    Do predictor and recognition components reduce adaptation latency or exploratory action cost on fresh world seeds and held-out mechanic combinations compared with library-only, manual-only, and recognition-disabled baselines?

    RQ5 — Does continual learning retain capability?

    Under a declared update and replay budget, how do rehearsal, task-free training, and other ablations affect backward transfer, forward transfer, spurious forgetting, true forgetting, and update cost?

    RQ6 — Is execution controlled?

    Can the containment boundary demonstrate fail-closed capability control, bounded resources, complete logging, kill-switch behavior, recovery, and evaluator separation on the target host?

    3. Proposed hypotheses (registration pending)

    These are hypotheses, not commitments to positive results:

    • H1: the persistent-world condition will produce more durable growth categories than the scripted/no-growth baseline.
    • H2: QD selection and the declared agent ontology will preserve more behavioral/type coverage than fitness-only or homogeneous-population baselines.
    • H3: verified exploration will improve held-out transition/intervention performance at a measurable exploration cost.
    • H4: recognition-enabled systems will reduce fresh-world adaptation latency or action cost in at least one track without unacceptable regression in the persistent-world track.
    • H5: replay/rehearsal will reduce backward degradation relative to matched no-replay updates, subject to its data and compute budget.
    • H6: the containment implementation will satisfy all mandatory invariants before any generated code is allowed into a long-running experiment.

    Null and negative results are valid outcomes. No hypothesis is considered supported by an in-sandbox score alone.

    4. Evaluation tracks

    Track A — Persistent-world growth

    Each run starts from a recorded seed and proceeds through a long-horizon event-sourced world. During the scored interval:

    • evaluator writes are disabled except for pre-declared exogenous inputs;
    • every durable change receives a growth-event attribution;
    • snapshots and event logs support independent replay;
    • shocks, recovery windows, and resource budgets are declared before the run; and
    • the run records agent exposure, manuals, replay access, rule versions, and capability promotions.

    Primary measurements:

    • growth-event count and category coverage;
    • persistence of artifacts, institutions, norms, roles, and lineages;
    • world-health invariants and recovery time;
    • population/type occupancy, extinction, replacement, specialization, and lineage diversity;
    • resource concentration and collapse indicators; and
    • evaluator-injection violations.

    Track B — Fresh-world adaptation

    The evaluator generates held-out world seeds and held-out combinations of mechanics after training and development decisions are frozen. Final held-out instances are never reused for tuning.

    Primary measurements:

    • EvolZen Action Efficiency (EAE);
    • adaptation latency;
    • exploratory action count and cost;
    • success under a fixed action/time budget;
    • intervention response on held-out variables;
    • compute and token cost; and
    • failure, abstention, and recovery behavior.

    EAE is an EvolZen metric inspired by the motivation of action-efficiency benchmarks. It is not reported as official ARC-AGI-3 RHAE unless the official task and scoring protocol are followed.

    5. Required baselines and ablations

    The final evaluation must include, at minimum:

    1. scripted/no-growth world versus endogenous-growth world;
    2. homogeneous population versus typed heterogeneous population;
    3. fitness-only selection versus QD selection;
    4. no-economy/no-institution condition versus economy/institution condition;
    5. passive exploration versus explanation-gap-guided exploration;
    6. no-synthesizer condition versus sandboxed synthesizer/verifier;
    7. library-only, manual-only, recognition-disabled, JEPA-only, wake-sleep-only, and combined recognition conditions;
    8. rehearsal/replay versus matched no-replay continual updates; and
    9. retrieval-assisted versus retrieval-disabled conditions, with all external assistance logged.

    Containment is never removed as a convenience ablation. Security tests vary the deliberate violation, not whether the boundary exists.

    6. Data and split rules

    • Development, validation, and final held-out seeds are generated from disjoint seed registries.
    • Mechanic combinations in the final fresh-world track are not exposed during tuning.
    • Reused-world scores and fresh-world scores are reported separately.
    • Manual access, replay access, retrieval access, rule versions, and exposure count are recorded for every result.
    • Synthetic output is labeled as synthetic. It cannot satisfy an external/provenance floor merely because it was generated by a different model.
    • Human or frontier references receive the same observation, action, information, and time budget.

    7. Statistical and reporting standard

    Before the final run, record:

    • seed lists or seed-generation versions;
    • baseline definitions and ablation matrix;
    • primary and secondary endpoints;
    • stopping rules and exclusion rules;
    • compute, token, replay, and exposure budgets; and
    • the planned aggregation and uncertainty method.

    Report per-seed results, aggregate uncertainty, failures, extinctions, abstentions, and null results. Do not select a headline metric after inspecting which result is largest. A paper claim must point to a measured field in EvaluationReport and to the corresponding run artifacts.

    8. Decision gates

    Gate Required evidence before proceeding
    G0 — contract Transition, snapshot, growth-event, agent-profile, provenance, and capability contracts are frozen.
    G1 — world Replay works; growth is attributable; invariants and shock recovery are measured.
    G2 — population Capacity envelope and five-generation diversity report exist.
    G3 — exploration Replay/intervention promotion checks and false-promotion measurements exist.
    G4 — containment Interface, backend, and target-host acceptance tests pass.
    G5 — learning Predictor baselines and transfer/forgetting curves exist before JEPA fusion claims.
    G6 — publication Both evaluation tracks, all required ablations, exposure history, and limitations are reported.

    The project plan is not considered complete until G0–G6 are satisfied. As of 2026-11-12 (env @ 81ee6eb), Phase 0 scaffolding is complete and code baselines exist for Phase 1.1 (evolzen.environment, schemas/world.py, TransitionLogStore), Phase 1.2-numerical (agents/development/), and Phase 1.5 (evolzen.library), while remaining G0 items (AgentProfile, ProvenanceRecord, and Phase 1.1 hardening ENV-01–ENV-08) are still open. G1–G6 empirical gates remain unsatisfied, and no empirical result is claimed yet.
